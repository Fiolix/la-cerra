Sardinia

Sardinia is the second-largest island of Italy and of the Mediterranean Sea. Located west of mainland Italy and just a few kilometres south of Corsica - another superb climbing destination - Sardinia is a typical Mediterranean island with hot summers and mild winters.

Caribbean-looking beaches, wild and untouched backcountry and mountains rising to around 1,800 metres above sea level make the island remarkably diverse. After a day of climbing, hiking, snorkelling, fishing or relaxing at the beach, visitors can discover Sardinian cuisine and the hospitality and openness of its people.

La Gallura

Gallura is a rocky region in the north-east of Sardinia. From the northern coast, Corsica is within sight across the Strait of Bonifacio. The region's history and culture are strongly connected with Corsica, and the local language, Gallurese, is more closely related to Corsican than to Sardinian.

The landscape is dominated by granite in almost every shape and size imaginable. Wind and weather have formed extraordinary stone sculptures, while the reddish Rosa Sardo granite creates a striking contrast with the deep blue sea. Cork oaks and the macchia - Mediterranean vegetation including myrtle, lavender, rosemary and thyme - complete the landscape.
